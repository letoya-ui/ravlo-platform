COMPETITOR POSITIONING

How to frame Ravlo against what they use today

vs. Spreadsheets & email

"You already have a system -- it's just made of spreadsheets and email threads instead of software." Ravlo replaces the patchwork with one place every role sees, and Ravlo AI reads that live data instead of someone writing a status update by hand.

vs. Salesforce / generic CRM

Generic CRMs track contacts and deals -- they don't know what a loan condition is, don't route to processors and underwriters, and don't generate compliance notices. Ravlo is built for lending and real estate specifically, so the workflow already matches how the team works.

OBJECTION HANDLING

Common pushback and how to answer it

"It's too expensive."

Anchor on what it replaces -- the tools, the manual re-entry, and the time spent chasing status across teams -- rather than defending the number in isolation. If the value case is solid but the number still doesn't work, that's what your discount authority is for.

"We already use Salesforce."

Ask what they've had to build on top of it to make it fit lending -- custom fields, workflows, integrations. Ravlo starts where that customization would have ended up, out of the box.

"Can we keep our current CRM?"

Ravlo isn't a bolt-on CRM add-in -- it's the operating system for the loan itself. Find out what they actually need to keep (contacts, marketing automation) versus what they're used to using out of habit, and scope the conversation around that gap.

"Why is there a setup fee?"

It covers real onboarding work -- account configuration, team setup, and getting their data in. You can waive up to your own authority if it's the difference between closing and not.

"Can we add workspaces later?"

Yes -- Additional Workspace is a standard add-on. Start them on the plan that fits today and let it grow with them instead of overselling capacity they don't need yet.

NEGOTIATION GUIDELINES

When to hold firm, and when to bring someone in

  • Lead with the standard rate card -- don't open with a discount that wasn't asked for.
  • Stay inside your own authority whenever the deal supports it; every approval hop costs time and momentum.
  • Loop in a Sales Manager or Executive before promising a number outside your authority, not after -- a promise you can't back is worse than a short delay.
  • Enterprise, white-label, custom development, multi-year, and strategic-partnership deals go to an executive by default -- bring them in early in the conversation, not at the signature.
